Thinking in Bets

Annie Duke 2024-03

🎲 Why I Stopped Judging My Decisions by Outcomes

— Notes from Thinking in Bets by Annie Duke

I used to think that good outcomes meant good decisions. If something worked out, I must’ve done the right thing. If it didn’t, I must’ve screwed up. It felt logical, even obvious.

But then I read Thinking in Bets, and everything shifted.

Annie Duke, a former professional poker player, explains that life is more like poker than chess. You can make a smart move and still lose — or make a careless decision and get lucky. That’s because there’s always some level of uncertainty, of luck, of randomness. And yet, we constantly fall into the trap of “resulting”: judging a decision solely based on how it turned out.

That idea really hit home for me. I realized I’d been harsh on myself for outcomes I couldn’t control, and too proud of wins that were more luck than strategy. I wasn't really learning from experience — I was reacting to results.

Another thing that stuck with me is how we rewrite history in our heads. Duke calls out the classic “I knew it” bias. We convince ourselves we saw it coming, even when we didn’t. It’s comforting, sure — it makes us feel in control — but it blocks real growth. We can’t get better if we’re not honest about what we actually knew when we made a choice.

The core lesson from this book is simple but profound: good decisions don’t guarantee good outcomes. And that’s okay.

Thinking in bets means accepting uncertainty. It means asking: What were the options? What did I know at the time? What was likely, and what mattered most to me? And then — just doing the best I can with what I have.

Since reading this, I’ve tried to let go of the obsession with being “right.” I focus more on the process than the outcome. I ask better questions, take smarter risks, and forgive myself when things go sideways — as they sometimes will.

This book didn’t just change how I make decisions. It changed how I see success, failure, and myself.

If you’ve ever felt stuck, doubted your choices, or been too hard on yourself for how things turned out — read this book. It’s not about poker. It’s about life.
